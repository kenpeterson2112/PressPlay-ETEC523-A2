Mission 01 · Foundations

Why audio?

Mayer's theory rests on three assumptions about how people learn from words and pictures. Tap each one and think about what it means for a listener with earbuds in.

People process sound and visuals through separate channels.

A podcast uses the auditory channel only. What could the visual channel add, and what could it overload?

Each channel can hold only so much at once.

A long episode with new vocabulary in every sentence can overload a listener. Pacing and structure matter.

Learners must actively organize and connect what they hear.

Passive listening is not enough. This is the case for questions before and after an episode.

Don't mix these up

Podcast: episodic audio, usually on demand.
Audiobook: a text read aloud, in full.
Lecture capture: a recorded class, often with slides.

Mission 03 · Questions that change listening

From passive to active

Two of Mayer's principles shape this design. Pre-training prepares learners with key terms and ideas first. Segmenting breaks content into manageable chunks the learner can pace.

Pre-listening questions

Continuous audio

Post-listening questions

Mission 05 · Students as producers

Make a two-minute episode

Podcasts are also something students can create. Use Mayer's principles as a design checklist for the task.

The task

Students plan and record a two-minute episode explaining one idea, applying at least three principles.

  • Purpose: one idea, one audience.
  • Script: chunked, conversational, no filler.
  • Voice: their own, or a deliberate choice.
  • Audience check: a peer listens and answers two questions.
